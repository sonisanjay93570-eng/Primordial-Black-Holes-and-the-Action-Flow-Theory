# Primordial-Black-Holes-and-the-Action-Flow
-Theory From Cosmic Structure and Energy Distribution to the Scale of Motion

Author: Sanjay Soni
Research Identity: Sanjay Soni Research
Research Area: Conceptual Science and Philosophy
Framework: Action-Flow Theory
Status: Conceptual hypothesis open to scientific review, mathematical analysis, observational testing, and independent verification.
1. The Central Idea

The Action-Flow Theory proposes a process-based approach to understanding the relationships among physical structure, scale, energy, interaction, and motion.

The central principle is that the size and physical scale of a structure can guide the investigation of its associated processes. Where significant energy transfer and interactions occur, the resulting physical changes and motion can be studied in relation to the structure's physical conditions.

In this framework, black holes represent an important research subject because of their extreme gravitational environments and their relationship with matter, radiation, and spacetime.

2. Primordial Black Holes as a Possible Early Cosmic State

Primordial black holes are hypothetical black holes that may have formed in the early universe under sufficiently extreme density conditions.

Their existence has not been confirmed. Nevertheless, their possible formation remains a subject of scientific investigation.

The Action-Flow Theory considers the following question:

Could primordial black holes have played a role in the transformation, redistribution, or organization of matter and energy during the early history of the universe?

This is a research question, not an established conclusion.

3. Structure, Scale, and Motion

The Action-Flow Theory uses the relationship between physical scale and motion as a starting point for investigation.

Its conceptual sequence is:

Physical Structure → Scale and Mass → Gravitational Conditions → Interactions and Energy Transfer → Motion and Transformation → New Physical States

The purpose is to investigate whether processes occurring at different physical scales can be described within a common conceptual framework.

However, physical size alone does not determine the magnitude of motion or energy flow. Mass, density, gravitational fields, temperature, radiation, and other measurable properties must also be considered.

4. From Black Holes to Cosmic Structures: An Open Research Question

The model proposes investigating possible connections among:

- Primordial black holes and early-universe conditions.
- Quarks and gluons and the formation of hadronic matter.
- Protons, neutrons, and nuclear interactions.
- Hydrogen, helium, and the development of cosmic matter.
- Gravitational contraction and star formation.
- Black holes and the subsequent evolution of cosmic structures.

These processes occur under different physical conditions and are governed by established physical theories. Their inclusion in one framework does not establish that they share a single origin or a direct causal sequence.

5. The Purpose of the Action-Flow Theory

The purpose of this conceptual framework is to investigate whether physical structure, energy transfer, interaction, and motion can be connected through a common process-based description.

The model seeks to develop testable questions about the relationship between the scale of a structure and the physical processes occurring within or around it.

For the primordial black-hole hypothesis, further development would require mathematical descriptions, comparison with cosmological observations, and predictions that can be distinguished from existing models.

Conclusion

The Action-Flow Theory presents structure, scale, interaction, and motion as connected subjects of investigation.

Primordial black holes provide a possible starting point for exploring questions about the early universe, but their existence and any role they may have played in cosmic structure formation remain unconfirmed.

This framework is proposed as an open conceptual research model. Its scientific value will depend on the clarity of its predictions, consistency with established physics, and independent testing.

Experience is an idea, not a claim.
